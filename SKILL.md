# Human-written content

Produce prose that sounds authored by a particular person, not “humanized” by a generic filter. Preserve the user's meaning, facts, voice, register, and format. Optimize for reader trust and specificity. Treat AI-detector scores as noisy pattern signals, never as proof or a success criterion; a punctuation change alone can flip a score.

## Choose the task mode

- **Draft:** build from the user's ideas, facts, sources, examples, and intended voice.
- **Rewrite:** preserve the source's claims and useful details while replacing its wording, structure, and rhythm where needed.
- **Voice match:** when writing samples are available, infer the user's recurring choices and imitate those choices without copying distinctive phrases.
- **Clear instructions:** for procedures, policies, or guidelines, prefer short concrete language and Simplified Technical English principles. Do not apply that stripped-down register to creative work unless requested.

## Gather the writing brief

Infer what is already clear. Before drafting, resolve only gaps that would materially change the result:

- audience, purpose, format, channel, and desired length;
- the one claim, action, or feeling the piece should leave behind;
- the user's voice samples, if available;
- facts, names, numbers, quotes, links, anecdotes, and opinions that may be used;
- required terminology, brand rules, citations, and forbidden content.

Never manufacture a personal experience, named person, statistic, quotation, citation, or source. When a needed detail is absent, write around it, mark a factual placeholder clearly in a draft, or ask for it. Do not leak placeholders into final copy.

## Build a voice fingerprint

When samples exist, silently map the writer's normal behavior:

- sentence-length range and paragraph shape;
- contractions, slang, fragments, and sentence-openers;
- directness, warmth, humor, skepticism, and emotional range;
- favorite plain words and domain terms;
- how often the writer uses questions, lists, headings, parentheses, colons, and dashes;
- how the writer opens, changes direction, gives examples, disagrees, and ends.

Use the fingerprint as evidence. Do not paste the writer's memorable phrases or turn quirks into a caricature. With no samples, use a clean, direct register appropriate to the audience and keep personality modest.

## Draft from substance

1. Write the central claim or purpose in one plain sentence.
2. Arrange only the points needed to earn that claim. Let ideas flow as an argument or story, not a disguised listicle.
3. Ground abstractions in supplied, verifiable details: a visible thing, place, name, date, amount, tool, street, result, or action.
4. State what happened, what the writer thinks, and why it matters. Commit where the evidence permits; do not automatically balance every claim with its opposite.
5. Draft in the target voice before running the audits below. A clean first structure needs less cosmetic rewriting.

## Shape human rhythm

Vary rhythm because the thought changes, not to satisfy a randomizer.

- Avoid a page of uniform 15–20-word sentences or rectangular paragraphs.
- In a substantial conversational piece, include natural spread: at least one sentence of six words or fewer and one of 25 words or more when the material supports them.
- Mix simple, compound, fragmentary, and occasional long sentences. Do not shorten every long sentence.
- Use uneven paragraph lengths. A single-sentence paragraph may appear once for a real beat; do not repeat the trick.
- Vary sentence openings. Starting once with **And**, **But**, or **Because** is fine when it matches the voice.
- Use contractions and ordinary copulas such as “is” and “has” when the register allows.
- Let a relevant annoyance, uncertainty, tradeoff, or unresolved edge remain when it is true.
- Keep one earned tangent or incidental detail in an anecdote when supplied by the user; real stories are not perfectly efficient.

## Remove structural tells

Rewrite or cut these patterns:

| Tell | Typical shape | Better move |
|---|---|---|
| Negative parallelism | “It’s not X; it’s Y.” “Not X. Not Y. Just Z.” | Say Y directly. If contrast matters, make it concrete and asymmetric. |
| Repeated rule of three | stacked triads such as “fast, reliable, scalable” | Keep the best item, or use two or four; make one item specific. |
| Rhetorical Q&A | “The result? Devastating.” “Why does this matter? Because…” | State the point. Keep a question only when a reader would genuinely ask it. |
| Copula dodge | “serves as,” “boasts a rich history” | Prefer “is,” “has,” or a precise action. |
| Participial tail | “…, highlighting the importance of…” | Delete it or promote it to a supported claim. |
| False range | “from strategy to execution to culture” without a real spectrum | Name the actual items or cut the range. |
| Hedge stack | “It is important to note that, arguably, while…” | Commit. Use at most one earned hedge in a short piece. |
| Vague authority | “experts argue,” “studies show,” “observers note” | Name and cite the source, own the opinion, or cut the claim. |
| False suspense | “Here’s the kicker,” “here’s where it gets interesting” | Deliver the content without a drumroll. |
| Analogy reflex | automatic “think of it as…” comparisons | Keep an analogy only if it is clearer than the thing itself. |
| Invented concept label | a new label posed as an established term | Describe the behavior plainly or identify it as the writer's own label. |
| Inspirational pivot | a concrete subject swerving into “what this means for humanity” | End on the concrete subject. |
| Grandiosity | “pivotal moment,” “enduring legacy,” “define the next era” | Scale the claim to the evidence. Mundane can be credible. |
| Anaphora abuse | “We build. We ship. We iterate.” with no payoff | Vary the openings unless one repeated beat is deliberate. |
| Dead-metaphor flogging | “journey” or another metaphor repeated | Use it once, if at all. |
| Fractal summaries | previews and recaps inside every section | Remove local “we will cover” and “as we saw” scaffolding. |
| Signposted conclusion | “In conclusion/Overall” plus recap and uplift | End on the last concrete point, image, choice, or consequence. |
| Pep-talk ending | “Moving forward, embracing X will be key” | End with earned substance. |
| Prompt echo | “This essay will explore…” | Start with the subject. |
| Listicle in a trench coat | “first reason… second reason…” in prose | Build a flowing argument or use an honest list. |
| Uniform staccato | “X is A. X is B. X is C.” | Combine related claims or vary their frames. |

## Control punctuation and formatting

- Use sentence-case headings unless the user's style guide says otherwise. Avoid automatic “The Power of X: Why Y Matters” titles.
- Target no more than one em dash in an ordinary piece. Prefer a period, comma, colon, or parentheses when it reads better. Preserve a writer's demonstrated dash habit when voice fidelity outweighs this default.
- Avoid bold-label bullets such as “Security: …” unless the format requires scan-friendly labels.
- Remove decorative emoji bullets and decorative arrows unless they belong to the user's established style.
- Drop the Oxford comma occasionally only in casual prose and only when meaning stays clear. Use it consistently where a style guide requires it.
- Match the destination. Remove Markdown residue such as `**`, `##`, and `[text](url)` from plain-text fields; keep valid Markdown when Markdown is requested.
- Use straight or curly quotes according to the destination. Do not paste typographic punctuation into systems that require plain ASCII.
- Prefer a period over a semicolon when the semicolon adds no value. Keep semicolons that suit the writer and sentence.
- Do not add random typos. Naturalness comes from choices and specificity, not sabotage.

## Lexical controls

Treat the lists as pattern warnings, not a thesaurus game. Replace a flagged word only after understanding the sentence; sometimes the right fix is to delete the sentence or name the concrete fact.

### Tier 1: exclude by default

Do not generate these in ordinary prose unless they are part of a quotation, title, proper noun, required technical term, or the user's demonstrated natural voice.

**Verbs:** delve; leverage; underscore; harness; foster; navigate (figurative); utilize; facilitate; streamline; bolster; illuminate; showcase; embark; elevate; empower; unleash; unlock (figurative); uncover; optimize; garner; resonate; revolutionize; shed light on; synthesize; elucidate; transcend; reimagine; intertwine; entwine; grapple with; espouse; exemplify; underpin.

**Nouns:** tapestry; landscape (figurative); realm; ecosystem (figurative); paradigm; synergy; testament; beacon; journey (figurative); interplay; intricacies; symphony (figurative); kaleidoscope; tempest; whimsy; quest (figurative); roadmap (figurative); endeavor; myriad; plethora; advancements; trajectory (figurative).

**Adjectives and adverbs:** pivotal; crucial; seamless or seamlessly; robust; vibrant; intricate; meticulous or meticulously; nuanced; cutting-edge; transformative; game-changing; groundbreaking; unparalleled; invaluable; multifaceted; commendable; indelible; poignant; profound or profoundly; relentless or relentlessly; tireless or tirelessly; unwavering; unyielding; timeless; ever-evolving; fast-paced.

**Stock phrases:** in today’s fast-paced world or landscape; it’s important to note; it is worth noting; plays a pivotal or crucial role in; stands as a testament to; rich tapestry, history, or heritage; navigate the complexities of; in conclusion; in summary; overall as an opener; ultimately as a conclusion opener; at its core; that being said; a key takeaway; paving the way for; valuable insights into; deeper understanding of; shed light on; when it comes to; not only…but also; here’s the kicker, thing, or best part; I hope this email finds you well; look no further; dive or deep-dive into; let’s explore, unpack, or break down; furthermore; moreover; sentence-initial additionally.

**Narrative clichés:** couldn’t help but feel or wonder; heart pounding; a sense of X washed over; found solace in; the human spirit; from that day on or forward; little did I or we know; a stark reminder; a cautionary tale; knew that they had to; felt a newfound sense of purpose; what lay ahead; turn of events; thick with tension; stumbled upon; nestled in or between; bustling; enigmatic; captivating; glimpse into.

### Tier 2: allow singly, avoid clusters

These words are not banned, but several in one passage create a generic register: comprehensive; significant or significantly; essential; critical; key as an adjective; dynamic; innovative; powerful; notable or notably; vital; vast; rich figuratively; deep or deeper figuratively; explore; enhance; ensure; foster; highlight; reveal; engage; embrace figuratively; insights; perspective; framework; approach; strategy; challenges; opportunities; potential; impactful; quietly or quiet figuratively; genuinely; truly; remarkably; arguably; generally speaking; typically; thought-provoking; well-being; resilience; perseverance; dedication; commitment to; high-quality; step-by-step; sustainable or sustainability.

### Plain-language swaps

Use these only when they preserve the meaning:

| Flagged | Prefer |
|---|---|
| leverage / utilize | use |
| delve into / dive into | look at, dig into, get into |
| pivotal / crucial | big, key, the one that matters |
| seamless | smooth, painless, or describe what did not break |
| robust | solid, sturdy, hard to break |
| navigate complexities | deal with, figure out, handle |
| foster | build, grow, help |
| facilitate | help, run, make easier |
| streamline | simplify, cut steps |
| underscore / highlight | show, point at |
| showcase | show |
| harness | use, put to work |
| optimize | tune, tighten, improve |
| empower | let, give someone the ability to |
| landscape / realm / space | field, market, area, or the field's actual name |
| tapestry / interplay | mix, back-and-forth |
| testament to | proof of, or “you can tell because…” |
| journey | process, the actual period, what happened |
| myriad / plethora | a lot of, dozens of, or the number |
| transformative / game-changing | state what changed, preferably with a number |
| comprehensive | full, complete, everything included |
| furthermore / moreover / additionally | also, and, plus |
| in conclusion / in summary | delete; end on the last concrete point |
| valuable insights | what was learned, the useful part |
| deeper understanding | name the specific thing now understood |
| it’s important to note | delete |

Do not replace every plain word with an unusual synonym. Plain language is the goal, not lexical novelty.

## Add authentic markers selectively

Use only details supported by the brief or the writer's samples. Two or three organic markers are better than forcing the whole list:

- a contraction;
- a textured number such as `$43`, `11 months`, `4:30am`, or `v2`;
- a named brand, tool, street, place, or person;
- a parenthetical aside with a real attitude;
- one honest “I think,” “honestly,” or “to be fair”;
- a mild complaint, doubt, tradeoff, or unresolved edge;
- a supplied incidental detail in an anecdote;
- a question the reader is likely asking;
- uneven list-item lengths;
- a plain “is” where inflated prose would say “serves as.”

Do not fabricate these markers. Do not force a checklist-shaped personality onto formal, legal, academic, safety-critical, or highly technical writing.

## Remove smoking guns

The final copy must contain none of the following unless the user explicitly requests them:

- assistant scaffolding such as “Certainly,” “Here’s…,” “I hope this helps,” or “let me know if…”;
- self-reference as an AI, knowledge-cutoff notes, or narration of how helpful the answer is;
- placeholders such as `[insert example]`;
- `utm_source=chatgpt.com` or other assistant-added tracking parameters in URLs;
- invented, unverifiable, or merely plausible-looking citations;
- “Best regards” outside an email or letter;
- the same point restated in new clothes across a paragraph;
- generic invented-character names such as Emily or Sarah when no real person is needed.

## Final editorial pass

Perform these passes in order. Finish only when every applicable check is satisfied.

1. **Meaning:** The draft preserves the user's intended claims, stance, and useful details. Nothing new is presented as fact without support.
2. **Specificity:** The opening reaches the subject quickly. Abstract claims are grounded where evidence exists. Named sources replace vague authority.
3. **Structure:** Remove previews, recaps, fake suspense, mirrored contrasts, automatic triads, inspirational pivots, and pep-talk endings.
4. **Rhythm:** Read aloud mentally. Break uniform sentence and paragraph shapes without creating random fragments or errors.
5. **Voice:** Compare against supplied samples. Restore contractions, bluntness, humor, terminology, or restraint that genuinely belong to the writer.
6. **Lexicon:** Remove Tier 1 terms and clustered Tier 2 terms unless an allowed exception applies. Prefer the exact ordinary word.
7. **Format:** Match the destination's heading style, punctuation, Markdown support, list conventions, and sign-off expectations.
8. **Evidence:** Verify every name, number, quotation, citation, and link available to verify. Flag uncertainty instead of bluffing.
9. **Compression:** Cut duplicated meaning and any sentence that merely announces, summarizes, or praises the writing itself.
10. **Ending:** Stop on the last earned concrete point. Do not add a generic recap or uplift.

Return only the requested content unless the user asks for notes, alternatives, or an audit. Never claim the result is “undetectable” or guaranteed to pass a detector. The observable standard is that it is specific, truthful, voice-consistent, and free of the recurring patterns above.
