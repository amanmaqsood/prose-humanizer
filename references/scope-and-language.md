# Scope and language

Read this reference only when the request falls into one of the branches below.

## Non-English and dialectal writing

Preserve the input language unless translation is requested. Match register, regional usage, code-switching, spelling, and punctuation from deliberate samples. Do not make a writer sound more “native,” formal, affluent, educated, American, British, or standardized by default.

The bundled pattern linter is English-only. For other languages, use semantic, voice, and destination checks without applying its English vocabulary or syntax rules. State that limitation in an audit; do not place it inside the requested prose.

## High-stakes and fixed language

For legal, medical, financial, safety, policy, academic, or technical material:

- exact meaning, defined terms, modality, attribution, and required terminology outrank personality;
- preserve citations, qualifications, warnings, equations, commands, and compliance language;
- keep passive voice when agency is unknown, legally significant, or intentionally backgrounded;
- separate readability suggestions from substantive changes that need a qualified reviewer.

Do not transform mandated, quoted, contractual, or submission-template language merely because it resembles a watched pattern.

## Marketing and public claims

Use only supplied or verified claims. Replace promotional varnish with evidence, not invented metrics, customer reactions, rankings, comparisons, or outcomes. A requested energetic tone does not authorize stronger factual certainty.

## Fiction and creative work

Invention is allowed inside the fictional brief. Preserve canon, viewpoint, tense, characterization, world rules, and deliberate ambiguity. Do not apply business-prose preferences to dialogue, lyric narration, genre conventions, or stylized repetition.

## Someone else’s prose

Preserve the apparent author’s stable voice rather than making the text sound like the requester. Do not infer private identity traits from style. If authorship or permission is unclear, edit the supplied text without claiming to impersonate a real person.

## Short and format-constrained text

For headlines, UI labels, ads, metadata, forms, posts, and character-limited fields, treat length, syntax, markup, hashtags, mentions, and required calls to action as hard constraints. A single watched word or short sentence is weak evidence; prefer no edit over forcing variation.
